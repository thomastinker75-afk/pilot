## Questions for the reviewer

1. Does the opening emergency instruction direct urgent danger to 999 without encouraging delay, evidence gathering or a website reply?
2. Are escalation routes appropriate for online sexual exploitation, sexual images, bullying, coercion and self-harm concerns? Does the distinction between listening support, advice and emergency response remain clear?
3. Is the image-handling exception prominent and consistent throughout the guide, including AI-generated images and material already on a device? Check the exact police wording and avoid asking people to send the site images or private messages.
4. Is the wording calm and non-blaming? Should the headline “Here’s the next hour” change to avoid suggesting a fixed triage protocol?
5. Should urgent but non-life-threatening mental-health pathways be added, with appropriate England, Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland distinctions? Do not assume one national NHS pathway covers the UK.
6. Are CEOP eligibility, Report Remove and Take It Down limitations described accurately? Are accessibility and language-support options sufficient for this audience?
7. Could the dictionary or message explainer make a parent infer abuse, drug use or suicide risk from an isolated term or emoji? Does it encourage conversation rather than diagnosis or accusation?
8. Does the website-support wording avoid implying confidentiality, trained staff or response times that the publisher cannot provide?
